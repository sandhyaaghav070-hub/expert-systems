01 · FOUNDATIONS

Intelligent System vs Expert System

An expert system is one particular, narrow-domain realisation of the broader idea of an intelligent system.

AspectIntelligent SystemExpert System
DefinitionAny system that perceives, reasons and acts to achieve goals across varied tasksEmulates the decision-making of a human expert in one specific domain
ScopeGeneral-purpose: perception, learning, planning, robotics, languageNarrow: one well-defined problem, e.g. diagnosis or configuration
Core techniqueSearch, learning, neural networks, probabilistic reasoningIF–THEN rules applied over an explicit knowledge base
LearningCan adapt and improve from data and experienceKnowledge is hand-engineered; it does not learn on its own
ExampleSelf-driving car, virtual assistantMYCIN (medical diagnosis), DENDRAL (chemistry)

03 · APPLICATION

Roles of Expert Systems

The same architecture is reused across very different jobs.

◎
Diagnosis
Identify the cause of a problem from observed symptoms — e.g. medical or equipment-fault diagnosis.
↝
Prediction
Forecast likely outcomes from current data — e.g. weather or financial-risk forecasting.
▤
Planning & Scheduling
Work out a sequence of actions to reach a goal — e.g. production or logistics scheduling.
◉
Monitoring
Continuously compare observations to expected behaviour and flag anomalies in real time.
≋
Interpretation
Make sense of raw signals or data — e.g. sonar signal or image interpretation.
✎
Instruction & Training
Diagnose a learner's understanding and tutor them accordingly — intelligent tutoring systems.

06 · RECAP

Key Takeaways

  • 01An expert system is a narrow, rule-based realisation of the broader idea of an intelligent system.
  • 02Its architecture centres on an inference engine that reasons over a knowledge base and working memory, mediated by a user interface and explanation facility.
  • 03Expert systems play many roles: diagnosis, prediction, planning, monitoring, interpretation, and training.
  • 04Knowledge acquisition is the iterative process of eliciting expertise from a human expert and encoding it as rules.
  • 05Shells provide reusable machinery for building new expert systems; meta-expert systems coordinate several specialists at once.
